Good Tidings Bible

Help make it better

The Good Tidings Bible is a new translation of the King James Version into plain, modern American English. It's being checked verse by verse, and suggestions from readers go straight into it: when one is accepted, everyone gets the better wording.

Suggest a change to a verse

Hold a verse while reading (tap more verses to add them), then tap Suggest. A form opens with the verses and their current wording filled in: write how you'd put it and why.

Suggest a change to a note

Tap a † or α mark after a verse, or open About this chapter, then Suggest a change to these notes.

Anything else

Something hard to read, a name spelled differently from one place to another, a bug in the app, or a word of encouragement.

How it's sent

Suggestions are posted publicly on GitHub, where anyone can read them and the answers. You'll need a free GitHub account to send one.

How to use this app

Reading

  • Tap ☰ to choose a book and chapter. Swipe left or right on the text for the next or previous chapter.
  • Hold the Bible tab at the bottom to switch to the Chronological Bible: the same verses, in the order things happened, a passage at a time. ☰ then lists eras and their passages, and marks the ones you've finished. Hold it again to go back.
  • The eye sets the look: match your phone, light, dark, gray or a photo of your own, plus text size and spacing.
  • Related passages at the end of each chapter lists other verses on the same things; tap one to go there.
  • The app remembers where you were, and works offline once it has loaded.

About the translation

  • The Good Tidings Bible renders the King James Version into plain modern English, checked against the Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek. Its name comes from Isaiah 61:1, the "good tidings" Jesus read at Nazareth as His own mission (Luke 4:18-21).
  • A small † after a verse means there's a note on it; α means one of its notes is about the original Hebrew, Aramaic or Greek. Tap it to read them. Tap a reference in a note to go there.
  • Notes on a whole chapter, psalm or book are under About… below the title.
  • Search also looks through the notes, so a word like hesed or agape finds every note on it.
  • To read without the marks, open the eye menu and turn off Translation notes.

Picking verses

  • Hold a verse to pick it. Tap another verse to stretch the pick to it; tap the first or last verse to let it go.
  • The bar at the bottom then offers Suggest (propose better wording), Bookmark, Copy and Related. ✕ puts the pick away.

Suggestions

  • Suggest opens a form on GitHub with the verses filled in. Say how you'd word it and why. You need a free GitHub account.
  • The Feedback tab sends anything else, and links to every suggestion made so far.

Bookmarks

  • The ribbon at the top drops a temporary bookmark where you are; the next tap moves it.
  • To keep one, tap Name on it in the Bookmarks tab, or pick verses and tap Bookmark.
  • Edit a saved bookmark to rename it, add notes, change its verses or delete it.

Search and going to a verse

  • Tap the magnifying glass and type words or a phrase; close matches show too.
  • Or type a reference, like jn 3:16, 1 cor 13 or Ps 23, to go straight there.
  • After jumping somewhere, ‹ Back to… at the top takes you back to where you were reading.
